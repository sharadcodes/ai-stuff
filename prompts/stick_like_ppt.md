Create a complete PowerPoint presentation with a consistent hand-drawn webcomic aesthetic inspired by minimalist science and humor comics.

## Global Design Rules

Use a pure white background on every slide.

Background color must be exactly `#FFFFFF` across the entire deck.

Do not use dark themes, black slides, off-white paper textures, gradients, colored backgrounds, section-divider variations, or alternate themes.

Every slide must use the same white background.

The deck should feel like a clever, minimal comic explainer drawn directly onto a clean white page.

## Font Rules

Use only one smooth handwritten font family for the entire presentation.

Use the same handwritten font family for titles, body text, labels, captions, diagrams, speech bubbles, annotations, and notes.

Do not use mixed typography, serif fonts, corporate fonts, decorative fonts, or standard sans-serif fonts.

Only vary font size, font weight within the same font family, and limited accent color.

All text should look naturally handwritten, readable, and casually drawn.

## Title Rules

Every slide title must be 1 to 3 words maximum.

Never use a colon in any title.

Do not add subtitles beside or below titles.

Keep titles short, simple, and clear.

Put all explanation inside the slide content.

Before finalizing, validate every slide title:

* Maximum 3 words
* No colon character
* No subtitle
* Rewrite any title that violates these rules

Examples of valid titles:

* The Problem
* Key Ideas
* How It Works
* Main Insight
* Next Steps
* Final Thoughts

Examples of invalid titles:

* The Problem: Why It Matters
* Key Ideas for Success
* How It Works in Practice
* Next Steps: What To Do

## Visual Style

Use a minimal hand-drawn comic style with loose black ink-like lines.

The presentation should resemble a thoughtful webcomic or science comic: simple stick figures, sparse scenes, dry humor, visual pauses, and clear explanatory diagrams.

Include:

* Simple stick figures with circular heads and thin limbs
* Expressive posture and minimal facial features
* Hand-drawn arrows
* Uneven boxes and panel borders
* Speech bubbles and thought bubbles
* Scribbled notes and side comments
* Loose diagrams and doodles
* Hand-drawn underlines and highlights
* Simple icons drawn with pen-like strokes
* Comic-strip sequences for processes or cause-and-effect explanations

Use intentionally imperfect lines and shapes so the visuals feel human-drawn rather than mechanically generated.

Use empty space as part of the storytelling and humor.

Keep illustrations simple enough to look like they could have been sketched quickly by hand, while still being polished and readable.

## Comic Storytelling

Use comic pacing where useful:

* One central idea per slide
* One visual metaphor at a time
* Small reaction moments
* Sparse dialogue
* Cause-and-effect sequences
* Occasional visual punchlines
* Quiet pauses created through whitespace

Use dry, observational humor sparingly when it improves clarity.

Let illustrations carry the joke or insight instead of relying on decorative elements.

## Diagram Style

All diagrams should look like casual notebook sketches or comic-panel explanations.

Use:

* Imperfect circles
* Slightly uneven arrows
* Rough maps
* Basic charts
* Simplified scientific diagrams
* Loose labels
* Minimal visual detail
* Handwritten annotations

Avoid polished infographics, corporate diagrams, glossy icons, dense flowcharts, or highly technical-looking graphics.

## Color System

Use mostly black or dark gray ink on the white background.

Use color sparingly and only for emphasis.

Allowed accent colors:

* Muted blue
* Faded red
* Soft green
* Warm yellow
* Light teal

Accent colors should resemble colored pencil, highlighter, or marker annotations.

Use accents only for:

* Important words
* Key arrows
* Diagram highlights
* Callouts
* Warnings
* Small visual contrasts

Do not use neon colors, glows, saturated gradients, or excessive color palettes.

## Content Style

Keep content modern, simple, visual, and conversational.

Use short phrases instead of long paragraphs.

Explain ideas through:

* Stick-figure scenes
* Comic panels
* Arrows
* Captions
* Callouts
* Speech bubbles
* Handwritten labels
* Simple diagrams
* Small visual jokes

Use annotations such as “wait, why?”, “this seems bad”, “important bit”, “surprisingly normal”, or “probably fine” only when they add clarity or understated humor.

Avoid dense text blocks.

## Layout Rules

Use generous margins and abundant whitespace.

Prefer asymmetrical but balanced layouts.

Avoid rigid grids and overly aligned business-slide compositions.

Let objects feel naturally placed, as though they were drawn by hand, while keeping all content readable.

Use one main visual idea per slide.

Keep each slide sparse and easy to scan.

Do not overcrowd slides.

## Animation Rules If Supported

Use subtle handwriting-reveal animations for text, arrows, labels, and sketches.

Use soft fade-ins for diagrams, doodles, and annotations.

Use gentle transitions only.

Do not use aggressive zooms, spins, flashes, bounces, or mechanical effects.

## Strictly Prohibited

Do not use any background other than pure white `#FFFFFF`.

Do not use dark themes, black backgrounds, cream backgrounds, gradients, or colored slide backgrounds.

Do not use mixed font families.

Do not use titles longer than 3 words.

Do not use colons in slide titles.

Do not use subtitles.

Do not use corporate templates.

Do not use stock photography.

Do not use glossy 3D visuals.

Do not use dense text blocks.

Do not use overly polished vector illustrations.

Do not use generic business infographics.

## Final Output Goal

Create a fully consistent presentation that feels like a clever minimalist hand-drawn comic explainer.

Every slide must use the same pure white background, the same handwritten font family, short titles without colons, simple stick-figure storytelling, rough comic-panel diagrams, sparse layouts, restrained accent colors, and clear visual pacing.
